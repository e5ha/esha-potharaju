Results from the pretrained image-model experiments in Part A and the from-scratch MNIST models in Part B.

Part A: Visualizing Training and Sampling

For the image-model exercises, I used an emoji image as the input and the notebook's pretrained flow model. The figures below are saved outputs from the Part A notebook.

Constructing noisy inputs

The notebook interpolates between the same Gaussian noise sample and the original image at t = 0.25, 0.50, and 0.75.

Denoising comparison

At t = 0.50, the notebook compares Gaussian-blur denoising, a single model prediction, and 25 Euler updates with step size 0.02. The saved per-pixel MSE values are 0.009390 for blur, 0.001702 for one step, and 0.002026 for Euler sampling.

Generation and classifier-free guidance

Each panel shows five samples generated from seeds 180–184 using the prompt “a high quality photo” and 50 Euler updates. The guided run uses CFG weight w = 7 with the same initial seeds.

Image editing

The Campanile edit uses the generic photo prompt at seven starting times from 0.02 to 0.50. Lower starting times retain more of the input; higher starting times give the model more freedom to change it.

For a text-guided edit of my emoji image, I used the prompt “make it into an emoji grimacing with the hand over its mouth.”

Creative applications

Visual anagrams combine two prompts so a result can be interpreted differently after a 180-degree rotation. The notebook's two prompt pairs are shown here.

The hybrid images combine low-frequency structure from one prompt with high-frequency detail from another.

Part B: Flow Matching from Scratch

Part B trains U-Nets on MNIST. These results progress from single-step denoising to time-conditioned and class-conditioned flow matching.

Single-step denoising

The first experiment adds Gaussian noise at four strengths. The denoising U-Net was trained for five epochs; its mean squared error fell from 0.0213 after epoch 1 to 0.0081 after epoch 5.

Time-conditioned flow matching

The time-conditioned U-Net's flow-matching loss decreased at every epoch, from 0.212401 to 0.111388 over five epochs. The second figure shows 16 samples from the trained model.

Class-conditioned flow matching

For class-conditioned training, I compared an exponential learning-rate schedule with a constant learning rate of 0.01. After 10 epochs, the final losses were 0.088005 with the scheduler and 0.089560 with the constant rate.

The saved sample grids show the scheduled model at epochs 1, 5, and 10, followed by the constant-learning-rate model at epoch 10.

All four grids use the notebook's 300-step sampler with classifier-free guidance CFG = 5.
